The quiet comeback of night trains

Sleeper routes are filling up across Europe. The hard part isn't demand — it's finding enough carriages.

By Mara Lindqvist · 12 min read

When the 21:40 sleeper pulled out of Vienna last Friday, every one of its 312 berths was taken. Five years ago the same train ran half empty. Operators say ridership on night routes has roughly doubled since 2021, to about 1.4 million passengers a year across 11 countries.

Travelers give two reasons: they want to skip short-haul flights, and a night on the train replaces a night in a hotel. Seats start at €39 and private cabins at €119, though popular summer departures sell out weeks in advance.

The bottleneck is rolling stock. Only two factories in Europe still build new sleeper cars, and most routes run on coaches refurbished from the 1990s.
